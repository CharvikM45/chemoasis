What open means here

Public code

Every page and every game is on GitHub. Each change goes through a pull request anyone can read, and our tests run on it automatically before it's merged.

✦

Suggestions you can see into

Your starting games come from a small model whose code anyone can read, and every pick shows its reasons. Its score is how well a game fits your answers, never a chance of getting better.

◎

Honest about evidence

Our games are inspired by tasks used in research with people after cancer treatment. Research results so far are mixed, and our own versions haven't been studied. We say that in the library too.

○

Your data stays yours

Open code doesn't mean open data. Profiles are stored encrypted, deleting your account erases everything in it, and the example posts you see in our code are made up and marked "(example)".

Where things stand

The picture isn't finished. Here's what's in place.

Updated October 25, 2026

In place

  • Six games, six training areas. Working memory, processing speed, planning, verbal memory, mental flexibility and hand coordination, each with levels.
  • Gentle by default. Story Echo, Finger Rhythm and Signal Match start with no timer. Challenge mode is opt-in, and its pace has a limit on how fast it can get.
  • A short questionnaire. Every question can be skipped, and you can change your answers any time.
  • Three starting games, with reasons. Play one or choose "Not for me", and your next suggestions follow what you actually pick.
  • A moderated community. Posts are labeled as experience or information, anyone can report a post, possible unsafe medical claims wait for a moderator, and you can ask for a second review of a decision.
  • Calm mode. One switch turns off the 3D effects and motion.

Being placed

  • Professional review. We're looking for oncology, rehabilitation and neuropsychology professionals to check our games, questionnaire and wording. Until they have, nothing here is labeled "professionally reviewed".
  • Quick feedback after a game. An optional tap or two on difficulty, enjoyment and accessibility.
  • A browsable library. Filters by training area and session length, and a place to save the games you like.
  • Password reset. This needs an email service first.

Still in the box

  • A phone app, built from this website.
  • Verified professional badges in the community, once we have a real way to check credentials.
  • Smarter suggestions, only after there's enough good data to test them honestly.
  • Research insights, only combined and de-identified, and only with consent, proper oversight and ethics review. Nothing about you is shared without your permission.

How a change happens

From an idea to the site.

  1. 01
    An idea

    It can come from the community, from the team, or from someone who's been through treatment and knows what recovery is really like.

  2. 02
    An honest verdict

    Before we build anything, it gets a score out of 10 and a check against what the first version of Chemoasis needs. Plenty of ideas stop here.

  3. 03
    A health check

    Every health fact needs a real source and says only what that source supports. Popularity and ratings never count as proof.

  4. 04
    A pull request

    The change is built on its own branch. The person who owns that part of the site reviews it, and the tests run before anything is merged.

  5. 05
    Watched after launch

    We follow reports and feedback, and fix or remove anything that isn't working for people.

The rules we build by

Things we won't do.

  • Call Chemoasis a diagnosis, a treatment or a replacement for your care team.
  • Say a game improves a medical condition unless research on that game shows it.
  • Share a health fact without a link to where it came from.
  • Treat popularity, ratings or "found helpful" counts as proof that something works.
  • Rush you. Speed and pressure are always opt-in.
  • Rank you. There are no leaderboards and no health scores.
  • Put anyone's real health data in our code, examples or tests.

Run it on your computer

You need Node 22 or newer and a Postgres database. Example content only: never use real health details while testing.

npm install
cp .env.example .env   # then set DATABASE_URL
npm start              # http://localhost:3000
npm test               # every test suite

Still open

Questions we haven't answered yet.

  1. Who should review our games, and what evidence should a game need before we call it reviewed?
  2. How should we check a professional's credentials before they get a badge in the community?
  3. How do we keep the questionnaire short and still make it useful?
  4. What should we measure to know Chemoasis is helping? We'd rather track ease of use, accessibility, safety and satisfaction than downloads.
  5. What would consent and oversight need to look like before any research use?

Have a view on any of these? Join the conversation in Ideas and feedback.

Chemoasis is a supportive platform, not a diagnostic tool or a replacement for oncology, mental health, rehabilitation or other clinical care.
