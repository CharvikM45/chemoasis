Research Studio

Turn your research into something people can play.

For clinicians and researchers who study recovery during and after chemotherapy. Bring an idea and the evidence behind it. If it passes review, it joins the Chemoasis library with your name and credentials on it, so patients anywhere can find you, not just where they're treated.

Now in preview. Every activity is reviewed by a person.

Why it matters

Research from experts anywhere, not just where you're treated.

During chemotherapy, so much runs on other people's schedules: appointments, infusions, the days treatment knocks you down. Brain fog, often called "chemo brain", can start during treatment, not only after it. The time between treatments and next steps is where neuroscience and technology can help.

Research Studio lets patients take some control back, during and after chemotherapy, with activities built on published research by experts anywhere. And it lets the people doing that research reach patients far beyond their own clinic.

    How it works

    From a study to the library.

    1. 01
      Sketch the idea

      Describe the activity, who it's for and what it practices. A design assistant turns your description into a visual mock-up you can refine.

    2. 02
      Show the research

      Link the published studies the activity is built on, by DOI or PubMed link, and say plainly what they found, including what they didn't.

    3. 03
      Confirm who you are

      Add your ORCID iD, your institution and, for US clinicians, your NPI number. We check them against the public registries.

    4. 04
      Share a prototype

      A playable prototype or a detailed spec. Submitted games run in a sealed-off sandbox, kept apart from players' accounts and data.

    5. 05
      Two-stage review

      Automatic checks come first: do the registries match, do the studies exist and say what the submission says, does the activity meet our accessibility rules. Then a person reviews it and decides. Software never approves an activity on its own.

    6. 06
      Published, with your name

      The activity joins the library with a short description, the research behind it and your credit card. Players can discuss it in the community.

    Your credit

    Your name travels with your work.

    Every published activity carries a creator card. You choose what it shows: your name and credentials always, plus your specialty, institution and general location if you agree. Players can follow the research links back to your published work.

    A "Verified" badge means we checked the credentials listed against a public registry. It doesn't mean every statement in the activity has been checked, and being a creator on its own isn't a medical qualification.

    Review standards

    What every activity has to meet.

    Honest claims

    The description says only what the research supports. An activity is never described as treating or improving a medical condition unless research on that activity shows it. If the studies were small or mixed, the listing says so.

    Gentle by default

    No timer unless the player turns on a challenge, a pause at any moment, and short rounds. Speed and pressure are always the player's choice.

    Accessible

    Large tap targets, nothing that depends on colour alone, no required dragging, and a calm version for people who turn off motion.

    Private

    Activities can't see players' accounts, and they never collect personal or health information. No leaderboards and no health scores.

    The submission agreement

    Five promises, in plain words.

    There's nothing to license and no fee. When you submit, you tick one box confirming that:

    1. You made this activity, or you have permission to share it.
    2. The credentials and research you list are real and are yours to cite.
    3. Chemoasis may host the activity for players free of charge, and adjust it for accessibility or safety, always crediting you.
    4. You keep ownership of your work, and you can ask us to take it down at any time.
    5. The activity doesn't collect personal or health information from players.

    This agreement is in preview. It will be reviewed again before we invite researchers widely.

    Questions

    Before you start.

    Does it cost anything?
    No. Submitting and publishing are free, and players never pay to play.
    Who owns the activity?
    You do. You give Chemoasis permission to host it, and you can withdraw that permission at any time.
    Is publishing an activity the same as running a study?
    No. Any research use of how people play would need their consent, proper oversight and ethics review first, and that's separate from publishing.
    What if my activity doesn't pass?
    You'll get the reasons, and you can change it and submit again.
