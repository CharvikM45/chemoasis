The side effect nobody sees

Depression is one of the most common effects of cancer care, and one of the least treated.

Up to 25%

of people with cancer experience depression, well above the rate in the general population.

National Cancer Institute ↗
27%

reported symptoms of depression during treatment, across 211 studies.

Krebber et al., Psycho-Oncology 2014 ↗
15%

still reported depression a year or more after their diagnosis.

Krebber et al., Psycho-Oncology 2014 ↗
Only 5%

of people with cancer see a mental health professional.

National Cancer Institute ↗

It rarely comes alone

Up to 75% of people notice changes in memory or focus during treatment, and up to 35% still do years later. Brain fog, fatigue and low mood feed each other: when thinking feels slow, it's harder to stay hopeful, and harder to reach out.

Janelsins et al., International Review of Psychiatry 2014 ↗

Our approach

Small steps that are easy to start and kind to keep.

Low mood makes big goals feel impossible. Chemoasis turns recovery into short activities you can finish today, picked for how you're actually feeling.

  1. 01
    Tell us how you're doing

    Ten short questions about your treatment, your energy and what feels hardest right now. Skip any of them.

  2. 02
    Get three games to start with

    Ranked for you, each with the reasons it was picked, so nothing is a black box.

  3. 03
    Train, a few minutes at a time

    Each game is inspired by exercises studied with people after cancer treatment (our versions haven't been tested themselves), and adapts as you play: harder when you're doing well, easier when you're not.

  4. 04
    It learns with you

    Every game you play, or set aside, tunes your next suggestions. Your progress is saved to your account.

Why this helps

Finishing something small gives a real sense of progress, and gentle attention and memory practice is a low-pressure way to keep working on the brain fog that often comes with low mood. The community is there for the part games can't do: talking to people who've been through it.
