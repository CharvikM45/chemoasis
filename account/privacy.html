Privacy notice

Your information, in plain words.

Chemoasis is a student-built project. Here is everything it keeps about you, why, and how to remove it.

What we keep

  • Your account: your email, the name you choose, and your password stored only as a secure scramble (we can't read it).
  • Your questionnaire answers: things like treatment status and what you'd like to work on. This is health information, so it is only used to suggest games to you.
  • Your games: which games you play, levels you finish and your scores, so you can see your progress.
  • Your community posts: anything you post in the community can be read by other people who have a Chemoasis account. It isn't public on the web. Your forum name starts as "Member" and a number, and you choose what to change it to. If you post anonymously, your name isn't shown, but moderators can still see who wrote it.
  • Open Research Studio activities (only if you make one): the activity, the studies you cite, your name, credentials, specialty, institution, location, ORCID iD or NPI number, website, mock-up and prototype file. Reviewers see all of it, plus your account email, to confirm who you are. A published activity shows your name, credentials, specialty, website, ORCID link and studies, and your institution and location only if you chose to show them. Your email and NPI number are never shown.

What's saved in this browser

  • A secure sign-in cookie that page scripts can't read, so you stay signed in.
  • Community posts you haven't sent yet, until you post or discard them, so a closed tab doesn't lose them.
  • Game progress, best scores and small settings like the 2D or 3D view, on this device only, so a game picks up where you left off.

Deleting your account also clears your unsent posts from this browser. To clear game progress from a device, clear this site's data in your browser's settings.

Checks and the design assistant

When you send an Open Research Studio activity for review, we look up your ORCID iD or NPI number and your studies in public registries (ORCID, the US NPI Registry, Crossref and PubMed) to check that they're real.

When they're switched on, the Studio's design assistant and its advisory read use Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic. Asking for a sketch sends Anthropic the description you typed. Sending an activity for review sends the activity, your name, credentials, specialty and institution, the studies' titles and abstracts, and the prototype or written plan, so it can point out concerns to the person reviewing. Your email, ORCID iD and NPI number aren't sent. Under Anthropic's commercial terms, it may not train its models on what's sent. Nothing from the questionnaire, your games or the community is ever sent to it.

Who can see it

Only you can see your answers, suggestions and scores. They are never shown in the community, never sold, and never shared, with the AI assistant or anyone else. Your community posts are seen by other members, as described above. The student team that runs Chemoasis can access the database to keep it working.

Where it's stored

On a secure database (Postgres, hosted by Railway). Chemoasis is not a medical service and is not covered by health privacy laws like HIPAA, so please only share what you're comfortable with. Every question can be skipped.

Deleting everything

Sign in, open Your games, and choose Delete my account and data. Your account, answers, scores and settings are deleted straight away, your community posts are taken down and their words erased, and any Open Research Studio activities you made are deleted, published ones included. To take down just one published activity, choose Withdraw on it in your studio.

Children

If you're under 13, please ask a parent or guardian to set up your account with you and to read this notice.

Not medical advice

Chemoasis's games are inspired by tasks used in published research, but they have not been studied themselves or clinically reviewed and are not a treatment. For new, severe or worsening symptoms, contact your care team. If you're thinking about harming yourself, call or text 988 in the US, or your local emergency number.
